ABOUT THE PROJECT

A research workspace for synthetic speech.

VoiceGuard is an academic field project developed by Kamulugari Abhinav. It brings trained speech detectors, audio playback, microphone recording, and saved findings into one browser interface.

What you can investigate

Check whether a recording contains patterns associated with AI-generated speech. Run detectors separately, compare their outputs, replay the source, and keep each result with its warnings. VoiceGuard does not identify speakers or perform speech transcription.

Three ways to bring audio

Upload a file, record one clip with Record Voice, or start live detection for repeated short microphone samples. Live results arrive after each backend response; they are not instantaneous streaming verdicts.

Supported recordings

WAV, MP3, FLAC, OGG, M4A, AAC, MP4, and WebM. Uploads must be under 25 MB and no longer than five minutes. The backend decodes the recording, converts it to mono, and resamples it to 16 kHz before model-specific preparation.

Check your recording ↗

What a result contains

The AI-speech score, model name, predicted class, audio duration, inference time, and applicable warnings. Window counts describe model processing, not a word-by-word or time-stamped explanation.

Your account and history

Use Google, GitHub, or email/password sign-in, or explicitly continue as a guest. Profile details and analysis metadata are stored in this server’s SQLite database. Signed-in history can be accessed on other devices using this same server; guest history belongs to a browser cookie.

Review, export, and delete

Search history by filename or model, filter by prediction, revisit a result, and delete individual records or clear your history. Export matching records as PDF, Excel, Word, or JSON from the history export menu.

How the application processes and stores data

The interface uses HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Three.js. FastAPI handles uploads and account requests; isolated Python/PyTorch workers run trained checkpoints. Firebase Authentication verifies accounts. SQLite stores profiles and result metadata, not raw audio or passwords. Audio is sent to the backend for processing, so use recordings you have permission to share with the server.

CPU inference can take time, especially while a model loads. A missing checkpoint or failed inference produces an error instead of a substitute score. Homepage 3D graphics and waveforms are visual aids, not independent detection evidence.

BUILT TO EXPLORE. HONEST ABOUT LIMITS.

A useful signal.
Not the final word.

VoiceGuard is an academic audio deepfake detection project. Compression, noise, unfamiliar languages, and new synthesis methods can affect model results.

Scores are experimental and uncalibrated. They do not prove a recording's origin or verify a speaker's identity. Keep the source audio and use independent evidence for important decisions.

A FEW THINGS TO KNOW

Before you press record.

Do I need an account?+

No. Open the sign-in dialog and choose guest access, or sign in with Google, GitHub, or email/password to link history to your account on this server. Guest history uses a private browser cookie; clearing it can lose access to those guest records.

What happens to my audio?+

The recording is sent to the backend for analysis. The user database stores profile details and analysis metadata, not raw audio or passwords. Only upload recordings you have permission to use.

Can VoiceGuard always identify an AI voice?+

No. Real speech can be flagged and synthetic speech can be missed. Results vary with recording quality and the material the models were trained on. Do not use a single score as proof.

Which models are available?+

Forensics, PellaV2, NII AntiDeepfake XLS-R Wild, and the official ProSDD ASVspoof2019 release have trained checkpoints installed in this project. Their runtime availability depends on the server’s checkpoint files and isolated inference environment. See the model explorer for preprocessing and source details.

What do the scores mean?+

A higher AI-speech score means that the selected model found more evidence for its synthetic class. Scores are uncalibrated model outputs, not a guaranteed probability of fakery. Different models can disagree, and fixed-crop models may miss events elsewhere in a recording.

Why can a scan take time?+

The server loads the selected model in an isolated CPU worker and then runs inference. Loading and longer audio add delay. Wait for the result or error; the visual animation is not a measurement of inference progress.

Is the homepage visual a live detector?+

No. The hero is an interactive illustration, and the listening section plays project test samples. Real model inference starts only when you initiate a scan in the dashboard.
